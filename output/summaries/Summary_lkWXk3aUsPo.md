## Overview
This video discusses the dysfunctional governance structures at the regional level in Indonesia, specifically focusing on the strained relationships between governors/mayors and their deputies (Wakil Kepala Daerah). The program features a discussion with a Deputy Governor, a Regent, and the General Secretary of the Association of Regional Deputy Heads (Aswakada), highlighting that the root cause lies in unclear regulations and a lack of defined authority for deputy heads. This creates opportunities for conflict, power struggles, and ultimately, hinders effective public service delivery. The discussion advocates for a clear resolution: either strengthen the authority of deputy heads or abolish the position altogether.

## Key Highlights
The central issue plaguing regional governance in Indonesia is the ambiguous legal framework surrounding the roles and authorities of deputy regional heads. The current laws, particularly Law No. 23 of 2014, are seen as insufficient and lacking detail, with provisions often being open to interpretation, such as the phrase "dapat dibantu" (can be assisted), which implies assistance is optional rather than mandatory. This ambiguity leads to a situation where deputy heads are heavily dependent on the discretion of their superiors, with their own authority not being independent. This often results in deputy heads feeling marginalized, leading to conflicts and a breakdown in the "dwitunggal" (dual leadership) concept, which was intended to symbolize consolidation. Evidence suggests a high percentage of these relationships are strained from the first year, a trend that has persisted over time.

The Association of Regional Deputy Heads (Aswakada) has been vocal in bringing these issues to the attention of the DPR (Parliament). Their input highlights that the strained relationships are often based on personal behavior rather than clear regulations. Aswakada argues that the current system, where deputy heads may be perceived as a burden, needs a decisive solution. They propose two clear paths forward: either eliminate the position of deputy regional head if it's seen as a liability, or significantly empower them with defined authorities if they are deemed essential for governance. This call for a clear legislative solution stems from the observation that without it, conflicts are inevitable and will continue to disrupt regional administration.

The discussion also touched upon the "distribution of the cake," referring to the allocation of projects and resources, as a significant source of conflict. It was noted that the laws do not explicitly regulate the division of assets or projects between heads and deputy heads, creating a loophole for misuse of authority and corruption, as evidenced by numerous cases of regional heads and their deputies being implicated by the KPK. While some internal regulations within the Ministry of Home Affairs (Permendagri) might exist, their effectiveness is questionable, especially when not adequately supported by primary legislation. The ideal scenario presented is a clear definition of roles and responsibilities within the law, or a system where the head of the region directly appoints their deputy, which could mitigate conflicts arising from electoral or political expediency.

## Implications
The persistent dysfunction in regional leadership relationships hinders effective public service delivery and can be a breeding ground for corruption, impacting public trust and the efficiency of governance. Addressing the legal ambiguity surrounding the roles of deputy regional heads is crucial for improving the quality of regional administration and ensuring accountability.
