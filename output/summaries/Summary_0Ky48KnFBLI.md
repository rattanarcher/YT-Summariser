## Overview
This video features an interview with Hadi Rudyatmo, former Mayor of Solo and a long-time PDIP figure, discussing his loyalty to Megawati Soekarnoputri and his perspectives on President Joko Widodo's political journey. Rudyatmo recounts his experiences supporting Megawati, highlighting her leadership qualities and his unwavering dedication. He also details his time as Mayor of Solo, contrasting his policies with those of Jokowi and questioning the narrative surrounding Jokowi's mayoral achievements, particularly regarding the development and promotion of a national car project. A significant portion of the discussion revolves around a mysterious payment of billions of Rupiah in cash to PLN (state electricity company) to avert a blackout in Solo during Jokowi's tenure as mayor, a transaction that Rudyatmo claims was overshadowed by the promotional success of the "SMK car" project.

## Key Highlights
Hadi Rudyatmo elaborates on his deep-seated loyalty to Megawati Soekarnoputri, tracing it back to 1986. He emphasizes four core qualities he admires in her: faith, steadfastness, courage, and patience. Rudyatmo shares a personal anecdote from the Reformasi era where he physically supported Megawati when her chair broke during a public event, preventing her from falling. He frames this act as a testament to his dedication and a reason for her continued trust. He also highlights how he remained in Solo as party chairman for five terms, serving 25 years, as requested by Megawati, prioritizing her directive over other potential political opportunities, even if it meant his personal sacrifice.

The conversation delves into Rudyatmo's tenure as Mayor of Solo after Joko Widodo moved on to become Governor of Jakarta. Rudyatmo claims he elevated Jokowi's previous policies, implementing a "3WMP" program focused on creating a wise, capable, well-fed, and well-housed society. Crucially, Rudyatmo reveals that Joko Widodo, before becoming governor, allegedly founded a political party named "Grindra" in 2009. He further clarifies that Jokowi's statement about not wanting to be president meant he didn't want to be *chosen*, but was prepared if elected. Rudyatmo also recounts his personal experience being displaced from his long-time residence due to a revitalization program, questioning the nature of the program and its implementation.

A major point of contention is Rudyatmo's account of the "SMK car" project. He claims that the cars were actually educational vehicles from vocational schools that were repurposed for promotional use during Jokowi's mayoral campaigns, a narrative he felt was misleading. He expresses disappointment that this issue was overshadowed by a significant cash payment of around 13 billion Rupiah from the Solo municipal government to PLN in 2011 to prevent a blackout. Rudyatmo alleges that this payment, which he personally drove and delivered in cash, was intended to be a secret but was obscured by the "SMK car" publicity. He speculates that this diversion of attention was a deliberate tactic to prevent negative news about Solo's financial management from surfacing.
