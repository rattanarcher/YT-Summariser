## Overview
This video features a discussion between host Akbar Faizal, Gerindra Party spokesperson and DPR member Kamrus Samad, and political analyst Professor Ikrar Nusa Bakti, regarding the perceived underperformance of 42 ministers and deputy ministers within the current Indonesian government. Samad outlines specific criteria for evaluating ministerial performance, including adherence to national development plans (RPJMN, RKPM), achievement of Key Performance Indicators (KPIs), and responsiveness to public aspirations. Both guests highlight systemic issues such as a lack of inter-ministerial synergy and weak coordination, suggesting these factors contribute to the perceived shortcomings.

## Key Highlights
Kamrus Samad, representing the Gerindra party, presented a critical assessment of the government's ministerial performance, stating that 42 ministers and deputy ministers are not meeting expectations. He detailed four key criteria for evaluation: alignment with the National Medium-Term Development Plan (RPJMN) and its annual work plans (RKPM), achievement of set KPIs, and responsiveness to public aspirations. Samad pointed to specific sectors experiencing issues, such as the Ministry of Energy and Mineral Resources (ESDM) for power outages and fuel supply chain disruptions, and the Ministry of Trade for policy contradictions regarding foreign investment versus domestic product protection. He also cited concerns in food security and mining permits, emphasizing that these issues negatively impact public trust and economic stability.

Professor Ikrar Nusa Bakti offered a broader perspective on ministerial effectiveness, emphasizing the importance of multitasking abilities, external relationship building with parliament and civil society, and inter-ministerial synergy. He suggested that some ministers might be proficient internally but lack external engagement skills, or vice versa, while others may struggle with coordination. Bakti also stressed the critical role of coordinating ministers in resolving inter-agency conflicts and ensuring cohesive government action. Both guests agreed that while specific individuals might have performance issues, the lack of synergy and effective coordination among ministries is a significant systemic problem hindering the translation of the President's vision into tangible results for the public.

The discussion touched upon the political implications of these performance issues, particularly concerning coalition stability and the upcoming political landscape. While acknowledging the need for political stability, the conversation implied that meritocracy and public welfare should take precedence over purely political appointments. The potential for a cabinet reshuffle was discussed, with an emphasis on replacing underperforming individuals with more capable ones, irrespective of party affiliation. There was also a mention of former President Joko Widodo's recent political activities and his alignment with the PSI party, suggesting potential political realignments that could impact the current administration.

## Implications
The critical assessment of a significant portion of the ministerial cabinet by a prominent political party and analysts raises questions about government effectiveness and potential policy adjustments. This discourse highlights public demand for improved public services and economic outcomes, which could influence future political strategies and cabinet composition, potentially impacting policy implementation and investor confidence.
