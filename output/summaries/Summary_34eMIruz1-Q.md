The video features a comprehensive interview between host Akbar Faizal and Muhammad Iskandar (Gus Imin), who serves as both Coordinating Minister for Community Empowerment and Chairman of the National Awakening Party (PKB). ## Key Highlights

The interview begins with extensive discussion of the government's newly implemented Unified National Socio-Economic Data (DTSN) system, which Gus Imin claims represents a historic breakthrough in Indonesian governance. He explains that this system, completed within three months of Prabowo's presidency, has resolved decades-long problems with overlapping and inaccurate social assistance data across ministries. The system now serves as the single reference point for all government social programs, eliminating duplicate recipients and ensuring aid reaches truly deserving families classified in the lowest income deciles. Gus Imin coordinates six ministries with combined budgets approaching 100 trillion rupiah annually, focusing on transitioning recipients from dependency to economic independence through job creation and small business development.

The conversation shifts to more sensitive political territory when addressing Gus Imin's recent public criticism of NU leadership under Gus Yahya. Acknowledging that his comments at a university alumni gathering sparked controversy, Gus Imin maintains his critique stems from genuine concern about NU's internal conflicts and missed opportunities for community development. He argues that NU should focus on education, healthcare, and economic empowerment rather than internal political struggles, while praising Gus Yahya's efforts to distance NU from practical politics but criticizing the resulting organizational instability that has left many leadership positions unclear or contested.

Regarding PKB's future and the 2029 elections, Gus Imin demonstrates characteristic political pragmatism, stating the party will support whoever is likely to win, specifically indicating support for Prabowo if he runs again as expected. He discusses PKB's electoral stability, noting the party has maintained consistent performance across recent elections despite facing challenges from money politics and voter pragmatism. When addressing his own long tenure as party chairman, he acknowledges the merit of KPK's recommendation for two-term limits but explains that internal party dynamics, including past traumatic conflicts, have complicated leadership transitions.

## Implications

This interview reveals significant tensions within Indonesia's political and Islamic organizational landscape, particularly regarding NU's role and leadership effectiveness. Gus Imin's public criticism of NU leadership suggests deeper fractures within Indonesia's most influential Islamic organization, which could impact both religious and political spheres given NU's massive membership base. His pragmatic approach to the 2029 elections, openly stating PKB will support the likely winner rather than promoting independent candidates, reflects the increasingly transactional nature of Indonesian coalition politics and suggests limited appetite for challenging incumbent power.
