## Overview
This video features Pontjo Sutowo, owner of Hotel Sultan in Jakarta, and his lawyer Hamdan Zoelva (former Constitutional Court Chief Justice), discussing their ongoing legal dispute with Indonesia's State Secretariat over ownership of the luxury hotel and its prime land in central Jakarta. The conflict centers on whether the hotel sits on state-controlled HPL (Land Management Rights) land or private HGB (Building Use Rights) land, with Sutowo claiming the government is attempting to seize both his hotel business and the building without proper compensation.

## Key Highlights

The dispute traces back to the 1970s when Sutowo's father was asked by Jakarta's governor to build the hotel and paid $7.5 million to the government for the land. According to Zoelva, the land was initially excluded from the HPL designation for the Senayan complex, but was later included in 1989 without notification to the hotel owners. The hotel has operated successfully for decades, paying approximately 80 billion rupiah annually in taxes and employing around 1,000 people directly and indirectly.

The legal battle escalated when the State Secretariat obtained a preliminary court ruling ordering the hotel's evacuation, despite ongoing appeals. Crucially, this ruling requires immediate execution even while higher court appeals are pending, but the state has refused to post the required 28 trillion rupiah bond typically mandated for such preliminary executions. Sutowo and Zoelva argue this represents an illegal seizure of both the land and the hotel business, as no court has ruled on the building ownership separately from land rights. They contend the government is effectively confiscating a private business worth billions without due process or compensation.

Both men expressed frustration that authorities have blocked event permits for the hotel, effectively strangling the business, despite no court order to cease operations. They argue this suggests ulterior motives beyond legal compliance, hinting at potential business interests seeking to acquire the valuable property and hotel operation through government intervention rather than legitimate market mechanisms.

## Implications
This case highlights broader concerns about legal certainty for investors in Indonesia, particularly regarding the complex intersection of state land rights and private property development. The dispute could set important precedents for how the government handles similar cases involving valuable properties built on disputed land titles, potentially affecting business confidence in Indonesia's legal framework.
