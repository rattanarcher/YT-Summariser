## Overview
This video from Forum Keadilan TV features an interview with Dr. Basroni Kiran, a former Indonesian presidential protocol official who served from the era of President Gus Dur to Susilo Bambang Yudhoyono. The discussion delves into the intricate protocols, decision-making processes, and key institutions involved in planning and executing Indonesian presidential foreign visits. Dr. Kiran clarifies the roles of various government bodies, particularly highlighting the crucial authority of the Minister of State Secretary and the Ministry of Foreign Affairs, and explains the financial and logistical preparations that underscore these high-stakes diplomatic endeavors. The video aims to demystify the behind-the-scenes workings of presidential travel, offering insights into its legal basis, strategic objectives, and operational complexities.

## Key Highlights
Dr. Basroni Kiran emphasized that presidential foreign visits are primarily driven by official invitations from host countries or participation in international forums such as the UN or ASEAN. However, presidential initiative, particularly for introducing a newly elected leader or pursuing national economic interests like investment, also plays a significant role. The entire process is governed by specific regulations, including Government Regulation No. 60 of 2018 concerning presidential and vice-presidential official travel and Law No. 9 of 2010 on protocol. The Ministry of Foreign Affairs (Kemlu) is identified as the leading sector for all foreign trips, coordinating communication with host countries through local embassies and providing crucial inputs.

The preparation for these visits involves a multi-agency team, including the State Secretariat, Ministry of Foreign Affairs, Presidential Military Secretariat, Presidential Secretariat, Presidential Security Force (Paspampres), and the TNI Strategic Intelligence Agency (Baist TNI). An advance team is dispatched to the host country to conduct reconnaissance, assess security, and manage logistics, ensuring meticulous planning. While the Minister of State Secretary holds the primary authority in determining the composition of the presidential entourage for foreign trips, the President retains prerogative rights to include specific individuals. Notably, during President SBY's tenure, members of the House of Representatives (DPR RI) and the Regional Representative Council (DPD) were routinely included in these delegations.

Regarding the funding and potential cancellation of trips, Dr. Kiran clarified that all presidential foreign visits are state-funded, with costs often running into billions of rupiah, and cannot be covered by private funds. Cancellations of foreign visits are exceedingly rare due to the extensive preparations and responsibilities undertaken by the host country, although domestic trips might be canceled more frequently. The former protocol official also shared anecdotes, such as the incident of Taufik Kiemas's lost passport during a trip with President Megawati in the US and a protocol breach involving AHY, underscoring the dynamic and sometimes unpredictable nature of managing presidential movements.

## Implications
Understanding the intricate layers of planning, coordination, and legal frameworks governing presidential foreign travel is crucial for stakeholders monitoring Indonesian diplomacy and governance. The emphasis on state funding and the rarity of cancellations for foreign trips highlight the strategic importance and commitment attached to these international engagements. Furthermore, the roles of the Minister of State Secretary and the Ministry of Foreign Affairs, coupled with the President's prerogative in shaping delegations, reveal key power dynamics within the executive branch and how national interests are projected on the global stage.
