## Overview
Tempo's "Bocor Alus Politik" segment this week reveals three significant findings regarding a planned demonstration on August 27, 2026. These findings suggest coordinated efforts and political maneuvering behind the planned protest. Specifically, representatives of the "Koalisi Pro Demo" (KPPB) met with DPR leadership the day before the planned demonstration, and a phone conversation with President Prabowo Subianto was facilitated through a DPR leader. Furthermore, a Gerindra politician was reportedly involved in encouraging local figures in Pati to participate in the protest in Jakarta.

## Key Highlights
The investigation by Tempo uncovered a series of events that suggest a degree of political orchestration behind the planned August 27, 2026, demonstration. A key finding is that representatives from the Koalisi Pro Demo (KPPB) engaged in discussions with the leadership of the DPR (House of Representatives) just one day prior to the scheduled protest. This meeting indicates a level of engagement between protest organizers and legislative figures, raising questions about the nature of their discussions and potential influence.

Further deepening the apparent political involvement, the KPPB representatives reportedly used the mobile phone of a DPR leader to speak directly with President Prabowo Subianto. This direct line of communication between the protest group and the head of state, facilitated through a high-ranking legislator, points to a significant effort to either negotiate, inform, or potentially influence the President regarding the demonstration. The specific content of this conversation remains central to understanding the dynamics at play.

Adding another layer to the alleged coordination, a politician from the Gerindra party, identified as Dipati, is said to have actively encouraged local figures in Pati, Central Java, to mobilize and participate in the demonstration in Jakarta. This involvement of a ruling party politician in galvanizing support for a protest suggests a complex web of interests and potential strategic calculations, blurring the lines between protest mobilization and political party activities.

## Implications
These findings raise critical questions about the extent of political influence and coordination behind public demonstrations in Indonesia. The alleged involvement of legislative leaders and even the President's office in discussions with protest organizers, alongside efforts by a political party to mobilize participants, suggests a potentially manipulated protest landscape that could impact public trust and policy-making processes.
