## Overview
This video features an extensive interview with Hasan Nasbi, who recently returned as Special Advisor to President Prabowo on Communications after serving briefly as Commissioner at Pertamina. The discussion covers his comeback to the presidential palace, President Prabowo's communication style and late-night media consumption habits, government policy explanations including the controversial school meal program (MBG), and defenses of recent regulatory measures on extremism. Nasbi provides insights into Prabowo's governing philosophy, his openness to criticism, and the administration's communication strategy amid ongoing public debates about various policies.

## Key Highlights

Nasbi explains that his return to the palace came through direct requests from Presidential Secretary Teddy Indra Wijaya and Golkar leader Airlangga Hartarto, emphasizing that he maintained consistent support for the government even during his eight-month stint outside formal government roles. He describes his new position as less operational than his previous Presidential Communications Office role, focusing more on strategic input rather than front-line public communication, working alongside a streamlined team of six assistants compared to his previous staff of over 100.

A significant revelation emerges about President Prabowo's media consumption habits, with Nasbi disclosing that the President regularly watches podcasts, social media content, and even critical commentary until 2-3 AM, including shows that harshly criticize his administration. This challenges perceptions of Prabowo being insulated from public criticism, with Nasbi noting that the President is well aware of negative commentary and uses this information to understand public sentiment. The President's willingness to engage directly with critics through roundtable discussions reflects his preference for transparent communication over traditional press conferences.

Regarding recent controversies, Nasbi defends the government's new extremism regulations by drawing parallels with democratic countries like the United Kingdom, which experienced major riots in 2024 following inflammatory social media posts. He argues that social order takes precedence over unlimited free speech, citing examples of how other developed nations regulate online discourse. On the school meal program criticism, he positions it within a broader 20-year strategic vision for human resource development, comparing it to post-war Japan's decision to implement similar programs despite economic hardship, ultimately contributing to rapid national recovery.

## Implications

This interview reveals the Prabowo administration's defensive posture regarding its communication strategy and policy implementation, suggesting ongoing challenges in public messaging despite claimed high approval ratings. The emphasis on social order over democratic freedoms and the detailed explanations of controversial policies indicate potential tension between the government's vision and public acceptance, particularly among younger, more critical social media users who form a significant portion of policy opposition.
