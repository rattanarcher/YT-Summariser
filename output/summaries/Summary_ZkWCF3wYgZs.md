## Overview
This Bocor Alus Politik episode examines President Prabowo's recent cabinet reshuffle, particularly his appointment of labor leader Jumhur Ridayat as Environment Minister just before May Day 2025. The hosts analyze this as a strategic move to appease labor groups amid economic concerns rather than addressing underlying economic problems. They reveal that Prabowo was concerned about potential labor demonstrations and chose to offer political positions to labor representatives instead of tackling the root causes of worker grievances.

## Key Highlights

The discussion reveals that Prabowo's decision to appoint Jumhur Ridayat was primarily motivated by fears of labor unrest during May Day celebrations. Intelligence sources and government insiders confirmed that the timing was deliberately chosen to calm potential demonstrations by labor groups, particularly the KSPI (Confederation of Indonesian Workers) led by Said Iqbal, who had originally planned separate protests at the DPR building and Gelora Bung Karno stadium. The government was particularly concerned about potential infiltration of these demonstrations by disruptive elements, similar to what occurred during the August 2025 protests that turned violent. Rather than addressing substantive labor concerns like revising the outdated Labor Law or improving working conditions, Prabowo opted for what the hosts characterize as "power-sharing" politics - offering cabinet positions to co-opt criticism.

The episode also exposes significant lobbying efforts by government officials to consolidate May Day activities into a single, government-controlled "fiesta" at Monas. Said Iqbal revealed that he was contacted by senior police officials and intelligence officers, and later met with DPR leadership and a deputy minister at a Senayan restaurant, where he was told the president was not pleased with plans for separate demonstrations. This pressure campaign ultimately succeeded in convincing KSPI to join the official Monas celebration, though some labor groups like the GEBRAK alliance (Workers Movement with People) refused and maintained their separate protest at the DPR. The government prepared extensively for May Day, with Prabowo personally approving the design of 200,000 t-shirts for distribution and planning to inaugurate a Marsinah Museum in East Java as additional gestures to the labor community.

## Implications
This analysis suggests Prabowo's administration prioritizes political stability over substantive economic reform, choosing to manage dissent through patronage rather than policy changes. The approach reflects broader concerns about economic pressures, including rising unemployment and a weakening rupiah above 17,000 per dollar, which could fuel social unrest if left unaddressed.
